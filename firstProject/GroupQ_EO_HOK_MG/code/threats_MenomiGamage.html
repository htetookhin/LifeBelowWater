OverFishing

Overfishing happens when humans catch fish faster than populations can replenish, so that too many adults are removed to sustain healthy reproduction and population levels.Roughly 35.5% of global marine fish stocks are now classified as overfished, meaning they are exploited at unsustainable levels. This widespread depletion not only reduces the number of target fish such as cod, tuna, or other commercial species but also destabilises entire ecosystems. When key species disappear or decline, predator–prey relationships get thrown off balance, weakening the food web that supports marine biodiversity. Making “life below water” more fragile and less able to adapt to the revovling world.

Habitat Loss

Habitat loss in the ocean occurs when vital environments such as coral reefs, seagrass meadows, mangroves, salt marshes, and coastal wetlands are destroyed or degraded more often by human activities like coastal development, dredging, pollution, and destructive fishing practices. Many species loose breeding grounds, protection, and feeding resources which trigger cascading losses up the food chain and dramatic reduction of species richness. The result is a weaker, less resilient ocean ecosystem, risking biodiversity collapse and long-term sustainability of marine life and human livelihoods that depend on it.

Invasive Species

Invasive species are non-native animals, plants or other organisms that humans introduce often accidentally into ecosystems where they don’t naturally belong. These species often spread rapidly because they face few natural predators or controls in their new environment competing aggressively with native species for resources and disrupting natural balances. This cause to build a marine environment that is less resilient, less diverse, and less able to sustain the full range of “life below water”

Pollution

Contamination of seas by harmful waste such as plastics and trash, to toxic chemicals, oil spills, agricultural runoff, sewage, and excess nutrients is known as ocean pollution. Many Marine animals from sea turtles and seabirds to whales, fish and coral suffer dire consequences by mistaing plastic for food, poison from tokic chemicals and entangling in large plastic waste leading to injusry and suffocation. Ocean pollution undermines the sustainability of marine life and ecosystems and ultimately, the people who depend on oceans for food, livelihoods, and ecosystem services.

Acidifcation

Ocean acidification happens because oceans absorb a large share of the extra Carbon dioxide (CO₂) that humans release into the atmosphere. Which causes a chemical shift reducing carbonate ions, a crucial ingredient marine creatures use to build shells and skeletons made of calcium carbonate. As a result, “calcifying” organisms like oysters, clams, mussels, sea urchins, many shellfish, plankton, and especially corals struggle to form strong shells or skeletons. Acidification can also influence reproduction, growth, metabolism, and even how fish detect predators or find habitat, threatening the stability of food chain. Harming entire marine ecosystems and altering ocean chemistry in ways that make the seas far less hospitable for many marine organisms.
